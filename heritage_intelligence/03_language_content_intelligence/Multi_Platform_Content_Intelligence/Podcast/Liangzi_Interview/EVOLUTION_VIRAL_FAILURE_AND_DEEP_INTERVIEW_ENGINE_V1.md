> HERITAGE RESEARCH ASSET. Learn mechanisms, not protected wording or a fixed visual template.

## 1. Evolution evidence
A public methodology retrospective reports that the 2020 starting form used relatively rough phone visuals, vertical single-camera framing, sub-one-minute duration and simpler emotional layering. Despite sincere interviewees, performance was weak and guest sourcing became difficult.

After more than 20 exploratory episodes, the team reports shifting toward story-led interviews, deeper excavation, horizontal presentation, more continuous editing and roughly 1–3 minute videos. It then reports an early ~300k-view small breakout and later million/ten-million milestones. These figures are creator/public-retrospective evidence and should be stored as REPORTED, not independently audited platform analytics.

## 2. Why the failure phase matters
The early weak phase is a high-value negative dataset:
- sincerity alone does not guarantee narrative value;
- short duration alone does not guarantee retention;
- vertical/native form alone does not guarantee platform fit;
- “走心” without emotional layers, scenes and change can remain flat;
- visual/technical friction can obstruct trust and attention;
- insufficient success makes subject acquisition harder, creating a supply-side feedback loop.

## 3. Breakout hypotheses to test episode-by-episode
Do not canonize these until cross-case validation:
- subject carries a clear contradiction or unusual life path;
- interview surfaces concrete story rather than abstract opinion;
- emotional movement has layers;
- edit preserves causality and progression;
- duration is long enough for payoff but compressed enough for short-video context;
- packaging creates curiosity without betraying the story;
- interviewer allows the subject to remain protagonist.

## 4. Deep Interview Engine

Candidate selection → Pre-research → Trust/consent → Opening safety → Fact layer → Scene layer → Feeling layer → Conflict layer → Decision layer → Consequence layer → Reflection layer → Meaning layer → Fact/ethics review → Edit → Platform packaging → Performance feedback.

### Question ladder
1. What happened?
2. Where were you / who was there / what happened immediately before?
3. What did you notice or feel then?
4. What was hardest or contradictory?
5. What choices did you think you had?
6. Why did you choose this one?
7. What did it cost/change?
8. What do you understand differently now?

## 5. Viral Case Protocol
Every verified/reported breakout must be compared with nearby ordinary/weak episodes from a similar era and format. Analyze Attention, Retention, Emotional Payoff, Insight, Shareability, Packaging and Distribution separately.

Do not label a person or sensitive life event “viral material.” The object of analysis is the content mechanism and audience response, not exploitation of the subject.

## 6. Failure Case Protocol
Classify creator-reported early failures and later weak cases separately. Test:
- subject fit
- question depth
- scene/detail retrieval
- narrative progression
- emotional credibility
- visual/audio obstruction
- title/cover promise
- platform fit
- distribution context
