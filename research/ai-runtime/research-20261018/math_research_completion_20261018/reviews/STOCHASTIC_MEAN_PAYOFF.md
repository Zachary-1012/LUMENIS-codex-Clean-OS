## Input, objective and output contract
Finite explicitly encoded total directed multigraph, MAX/MIN/CHANCE partition; integer edge rewards; binary rational nonnegative chance probabilities summing to one at each chance vertex. Loops, parallel edges, zero probabilities and unreduced fractions are allowed. Payoff is the expectation of the pathwise liminf average, with arbitrary history-dependent behavioral opponents. Output is every vertex whose value is at least zero, including exact equality. The manuscript claims uniform deterministic bit complexity 2^{C log²(L+2)}. It does not give a user-facing strategy-output contract or statistical estimation guarantee for learned transition probabilities.

## Claim and dependency ledger
1. **Section 2, quantitative discounted bridge.** Set Q to the product of supplied chance denominators, W=max(1,max|w|), A0=n![Q(3+W)]^n, H=(W+4)A0. The normalized discounted Bellman map is max/min/expectation of u*w+(1-u)t_j. Fixed-policy transition probabilities sum parallel edges, while rewards are averaged separately. Cramer's numerator and denominator coefficient norms are bounded by A0; edge-comparison polynomial norms by H. The first nonzero coefficient controls the sign for 0<u<=1/(2H). Choosing an attaining pair at lambda0=1/(32H²) gives a proof witness, not a step requiring the algorithm to enumerate all policies.
2. **Value gap and approximation.** Cancel common powers of u in each rational discounted value. The constant term G has denominator at most A0; a nonzero G has magnitude at least 1/H. The discounted approximation error at lambda0 is at most 1/(16H). The next coefficient gives a bias relation on value-preserving edges.
3. **Behavioral-strategy bridge.** Under the selected MAX policy, G along play is a finite-valued submartingale; it eventually stabilizes. Square-increment summability and bounded martingale-difference averaging justify the pathwise limit statement. Bias telescoping proves the lower bound against arbitrary MIN behavior; the reverse argument proves the upper bound. Interchanging expectation and liminf without this argument would be invalid.
4. **Section 3, abstract simultaneous labeling.** F must be monotone and satisfy F(t+c1)=F(t)+gamma*c1 with 0<gamma<1. Integer witnesses in an integer box satisfy inequalities only on their respective supports. Starting at the upper corner, iterate clip(floor(F(t))). The descending integer potential gives at most nD+1 evaluations. This is an integer comparison construction, not ordinary continuous fixed-point iteration.
5. **Width representation and recursion.** D=h*2^d, 128<=h<=255, d>=0. The mass base applies first when every a_i*b_i>1. The width base is d<12, **not D<4096**; it permits widths up to 255*2048=522240. Nonbase calls use center D/2, range D/8, narrow width D/128 and step D/4096. Downward and upward updates are simultaneous; strict I-minus/I-plus tests and the final negative override matter. Both passes include their unchanged terminal update. Rational factors 4/3, 4/5 and 8/5 must remain exact.
6. **Translation argument.** The plus witness shift is negative, the minus witness shift positive; discounting changes the inequalities in opposite ways. An undiscouted translation proof cannot simply be reused. The rank is d+B, where B is the smallest j with (32/25)^j*min_i(a_i*b_i)>1. Only one half-width child can preserve B; the remaining children reduce it. This yields the claimed quasipolynomial tree bound, not a small numerical constant.
7. **Section 4, scaling.** N=32H², K=256(N+1), S=16HK, J=SW+N+1. Use F(t)=S*f_{1/N}(t/S). Proof-only integer bounds Y=floor(Sv)-N and Z=ceil(Sv)+N have gap <=K/128. They are not provided to the algorithm.
8. **Outer box algorithm.** Start A=-J1, D0=128*2^d0>=2J. While D>K call labeling with uniform masses 1/n. A plus label means Z_i>A_i+D/32; a minus label means Y_i<A_i+D-D/32. These are contrapositives of witness guarantees, not winner labels. Move to the next smaller allowed width: (h-1)2^d for h>128, otherwise 255*2^(d-1). Increase only plus lower endpoints by delta=D-D'. The gap and strips retain both Y and Z. At most 128(d0+1) outer calls.
9. **Exact final decision.** A_i/S approximates value within 1/(8H); output A_i>=-8K, equivalently A_i/S>=-1/(2H). Testing A_i>=0 would incorrectly exclude some zero-valued states. Visual page 18 confirms both absolute-value error bounds and the negative threshold.
10. **Bit implementation.** At chance vertices exact denominator QN and numerator sum_e c_e*(Q/d_e)*(S*w_e+(N-1)t_j). All input denominators divide Q even if unreduced. Flooring a negative rational must use mathematical floor, not truncation. The proof's polynomial bit lengths do not imply acceptable wall time.
11. **Reachability application.** Absorbing target reward +1 and all other edges -1 yield payoff 2*Pr(hit)-1. This reduction requires the specified absorbing construction; an arbitrary transient target reward does not implement it.
