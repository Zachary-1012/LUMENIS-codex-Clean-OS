# KOS MVP Operating Playbook — Hainan CDF

## Objective

Today-first objective: produce a usable KOS monitoring result from existing Z Brand Core capabilities. Do not introduce a parallel KOS platform or duplicate the Core.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Original platform content/data
2. Verified account/profile information
3. Core-derived metrics
4. AI semantic enrichment
5. Human correction

AI may enrich classification but must not override subject boundaries, raw counts, dates, or evidence.

## KOS classification

A KOS candidate is a public propagation subject that has evidence of brand/business relevance and repeated or professionalized content behavior. Employment, sales role, official affiliation, or authorization must never be inferred without evidence.

Identity labels remain separate from content labels:

- KOS / KOL / KOC / UGC / MEDIA / OFFICIAL / UNKNOWN
- Content: experience / review / recommendation / activity / complaint / commercial / official / other

## S/A/B/C operating layer

S: benchmark subjects; prioritize observation, case study, and controlled amplification candidates.

A: high-value subjects; prioritize cultivation, topic/task opportunities, and content feedback.

B: stable subjects; maintain low-cost recurring observation and lightweight activation.

C: candidate/low-priority subjects; observe, test activation, or leave dormant.

Tier is relative to the current monitored sample and must be recalculated as data changes.

## Content amplification logic

Natural performance first. A high-performing post becomes an amplification candidate only when relevance is high, risk is acceptable, and there is evidence of consumer intent. The system must distinguish "recommended for amplification" from "actually boosted" and must not invent spend, conversion, or ROI.

## Incentive logic

Use internal points only for analytical simulation until real business rules are supplied. Candidate dimensions: valid publication, relevance, content quality, engagement, reach, event contribution, and benchmark contribution. Negative signals include policy violations, misleading claims, unsupported affiliation, and high-risk content.

## Daily operating loop

Collect/import → normalize → subject boundary → deduplicate → identify author → classify identity → link content → calculate metrics → tier → detect events/needs → risk review → action recommendation → report → evidence.

## Hainan CDF scope

Primary monitoring scope is China Tourism Group Duty Free / CDF and its clearly evidenced Hainan entities. Important entities include Sanya International Duty Free City, Haikou International Duty Free City, and CDF Sanya urban duty-free operations. Other Hainan duty-free operators must not be merged into CDF without evidence.

## Required result

The MVP is complete only when it can produce: total KOS candidates, S/A/B/C distribution, top subjects, top content, emerging subjects, risk subjects, content topics, consumer needs, event links, brand links, recommended actions, and traceable source evidence.

## External operating lessons adopted

- Official Xiaohongshu commercial ecosystem separates creator selection, cooperation, content amplification, and post-campaign review.
- KOS programs work better when content, process, and incentives are designed together rather than relying on an account-management tool alone.
- Tiered incentives should reward quality and outcomes progressively instead of rewarding volume only.
- Strong natural content can be selected for paid amplification; amplification status must remain a separate fact field.

These are operating patterns, not claims that the monitored brand currently uses them.
